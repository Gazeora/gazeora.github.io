Features

Everything between your game and your eyes

Gazeora is a game launcher, a capture engine and a stream server in one window.

VR viewer mode

Each eye gets its own picture with barrel pre-distortion and color-fringe correction for your viewer's lenses. Tune distortion, size and lens separation with the headset on; changes show at once. On by default.

3D from depth

In Direct3D 11 and 12 games, the hook copies the game's depth buffer and Gazeora renders a separate view for each eye from it, so the scene has real depth in the viewer, not just two copies of a flat image.

Low latency

Frames stay on the GPU from capture to encoder. Packets go out on their own thread while the next frame encodes, and nothing queues up behind a slow one.

Hardware encoding

H.264 or H.265 on the GPU's encoder (NVIDIA NVENC, AMD AMF or Intel Quick Sync). H.265 gives the same picture at 70% of the bits, so Wi-Fi has fewer packets to lose.

Frame capture

A small hook DLL copies each presented frame as a shared GPU texture. Works with Direct3D 8, 9, 10, 11, 12 and OpenGL games.

Built for Wi-Fi

Frames are cut into small slices and packets are paced, not burst. A lost packet smears a strip briefly instead of freezing the picture, and the bitrate adapts to loss.

Hand gestures

A webcam turns your hands into a touch screen in the air: point to move, fold a finger to click, pinch to scroll. MediaPipe hand models run on the CPU.

Touch from your phone

With VR mode off, taps on the picture click the Gazeora window and swipes scroll it, without moving the PC's own mouse or touching other programs.

Found automatically

The Gazeora Android app finds your PC on the network by itself, so nobody types an address. Or plug in a USB cable instead.

Plays in VLC, ffplay, OBS

Standard RTSP and MPEG-TS over HTTP, so any player on any device opens the stream without extra software.

Game library

Add games by exe or shortcut, launch them directly or through Steam, and track play time. Follows games that restart through Steam, Ubisoft Connect or the EA app.

Command line

GazeoraCLI manages the same library and can attach to a game that is already running.

How it works

From the game's GPU to your screen, without a detour

  1. 1

    Launch

    Gazeora starts the game suspended, or attaches to it if it's already running, and injects gazeora_hook.dll.

  2. 2

    Capture

    The hook finds the graphics API, hooks its present call and copies each frame into a ring of shared textures. GPU fences order the copies without stalling the game.

  3. 3

    Prepare

    The launcher scales the frame on the GPU, applies the VR layout and converts it to NV12, all without leaving video memory.

  4. 4

    Stream

    The GPU's encoder (NVENC, AMF or Quick Sync) encodes H.264 or H.265 straight from the texture, and packets go out to everyone watching.

Good to know

  • Games with kernel-level anti-cheat may block capture. Don't use it with online games whose terms forbid it.
  • The stream is unencrypted: anyone on your local network can watch it.
  • Vulkan games aren't supported yet.
  • Windows N editions need the Media Feature Pack for H.264.
